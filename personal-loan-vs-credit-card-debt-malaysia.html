Personal Loan vs Credit Card Debt: Which One Costs More in Malaysia?

Last updated: May 2026  |  Reading time: ~7 min

Quick Answer Credit card debt in Malaysia almost always costs more than a personal loan. Credit cards charge up to 18% interest per year on unpaid balances under Bank Negara Malaysia's regulated cap, while personal loans from banks typically range from 5% to 12% per year (flat rate). Carrying even RM5,000 in credit card debt for one year can cost over RM900 in interest alone — compared to far less with a structured personal loan at a lower rate.

The Core Difference: How Interest Is Calculated

To understand which type of debt costs more, you need to understand how interest is charged on each one.

Credit Card Interest in Malaysia

If you do not pay your credit card balance in full each month, the unpaid amount is subject to a daily compounding finance charge. In Malaysia, Bank Negara Malaysia caps credit card interest at 18% per annum. However, this is applied on your daily outstanding balance — and it compounds. This means you pay interest on interest, which accelerates the total cost quickly.

For example, if you have RM5,000 outstanding on your credit card and only make minimum payments of around RM125 per month (2.5%), it would take more than five years to clear the balance, and you would pay nearly RM3,500 in interest on top of the original RM5,000.

Important: Credit card minimum payments in Malaysia are typically 2% to 5% of the outstanding balance or RM50, whichever is higher. Paying only the minimum is one of the most expensive financial habits in Malaysia.

Personal Loan Interest in Malaysia

Personal loans from Malaysian banks are usually structured with a flat interest rate applied to the original loan amount. A common range is 5% to 12% per annum flat rate, depending on the lender, your income, and your credit profile.

With a flat rate structure, the total interest is calculated upfront and divided equally over the repayment period. This gives you a fixed monthly instalment and a clear end date — which makes budgeting much easier than revolving credit card debt.

For a RM10,000 personal loan at 7% flat rate over 3 years: total interest = RM10,000 × 7% × 3 = RM2,100. Monthly repayment ≈ RM338. Total paid = RM12,100.

How Credit Card Debt Affects Your Loan Application

When you apply for a home loan, car loan, or any other credit facility in Malaysia, the bank checks your total monthly commitments. Credit card debt affects this in two ways:

1. Minimum Payment Counts as a Commitment

Banks typically count 5% of your total credit card limit — or the actual monthly payment, whichever is higher — as a monthly commitment for DSR calculation. This is true even if you pay your balance in full each month and even if you rarely use the card.

2. Payment History Appears in CCRIS

Any late payments on your credit card will show up in your CCRIS report, which every Malaysian bank checks before approving a loan. Multiple late payments over 12 months can be a red flag for credit officers.

When Does Debt Consolidation Make Sense?

If you are carrying RM10,000 or more in high-interest credit card debt, a debt consolidation personal loan can reduce your total interest cost and simplify your repayments into a single fixed monthly instalment.

However, consolidation only works if you stop using the credit card as a borrowing tool afterwards. Otherwise, you end up with both the personal loan and a new accumulating credit card balance — which is worse than before.

If your debt situation is severe and you are struggling to meet minimum payments, consider reaching out to AKPK (Agensi Kaunseling dan Pengurusan Kredit), which offers free financial counselling and a structured Debt Management Programme.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 18% the maximum credit card interest rate in Malaysia?

Yes. Bank Negara Malaysia has capped credit card finance charges at 18% per annum for all licensed banks in Malaysia. However, this is applied on a daily compounding basis on your outstanding balance, which means the effective cost of carrying a balance is very high over time.

Can I use a personal loan to pay off credit card debt in Malaysia?

Yes, this is called debt consolidation. It typically makes financial sense if your personal loan interest rate is significantly lower than 18%, which is common for borrowers with good credit. The key is to close or reduce the credit card limit afterwards to avoid accumulating new debt.

How does credit card debt affect my home loan application?

Banks in Malaysia include a percentage of your total credit card limit (typically 5%) as a monthly commitment in your DSR calculation. High credit card limits — even if unpaid — reduce your effective borrowing capacity for a home loan. Late credit card payments also appear in your CCRIS report.

What is a flat interest rate on a personal loan?

A flat interest rate means interest is calculated on the original loan amount for the entire loan period. For example, a RM10,000 loan at 7% flat for 3 years means you pay RM700 per year in interest regardless of how much principal you have already repaid. This differs from a reducing balance method used in most home loans.

What is the minimum payment on a Malaysian credit card?

In Malaysia, credit card issuers typically require a minimum monthly payment of either 2% to 5% of the outstanding balance, or RM50, whichever is higher. Paying only the minimum significantly extends the repayment period and dramatically increases the total interest cost.

Is it better to have a personal loan or credit card debt on my CCRIS?

A personal loan with consistent, on-time payments generally looks more positive on a CCRIS report because it demonstrates structured repayment behaviour. A revolving credit card balance with late payments is a bigger red flag. However, both will appear on your CCRIS, so maintaining good payment history on either product is important.
