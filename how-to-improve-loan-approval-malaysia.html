How to Improve Loan Approval Chances in Malaysia

Last updated: May 2026  |  Reading time: ~7 min

Quick Answer To improve your loan approval chances in Malaysia, focus on six areas: clear your CCRIS record, lower your Debt Service Ratio (DSR) below 60%, settle outstanding credit card balances, maintain stable employment for at least six months, avoid multiple loan applications within a short period, and check your CTOS score before applying. These steps can meaningfully strengthen your application at any Malaysian bank.
This guide is for general information only. It is not financial advice and is not affiliated with any bank or government agency. Always consult the bank directly for the latest lending criteria.

Why Banks Reject Loan Applications

Getting a loan rejected is stressful — especially when you genuinely need the financing. But most rejections are not random. Banks follow a structured credit assessment process, and understanding what they look for gives you a real advantage before you apply.

Malaysian banks assess several key areas: your income stability, your existing debt commitments, your credit history, and whether the loan amount is realistic given your financial profile. A weakness in any one of these areas can lead to a rejection or a lower approved amount than you hoped for.

The good news is that most of these factors are within your control. With some preparation — ideally three to six months before you plan to apply — you can address the common weaknesses and walk into the application with a much stronger profile.

Step-by-Step: Strengthening Your Loan Application

1 Check Your CCRIS Report First

Your CCRIS (Central Credit Reference Information System) report is managed by Bank Negara Malaysia and shows your full borrowing history across all banks. This is one of the first things any Malaysian bank checks when you submit a loan application.

The report shows how consistently you have been making repayments. Even one or two late payments in the past 12 months can raise a red flag. You can request a free CCRIS report at any Bank Negara branch or through the eCCRIS portal online.

Once you have the report, look for: any accounts marked as in arrears, any outstanding overdue amounts, and the total number of active credit facilities in your name. Read our full CCRIS report guide to understand what each section means.

Tip: If you find an error in your CCRIS report — such as a loan you have already settled still showing as active — contact the bank that issued the credit to have it corrected before you apply.

2 Reduce Your Debt Service Ratio (DSR)

DSR is the percentage of your gross or net monthly income that goes toward servicing all your existing loan repayments. Most Malaysian banks cap DSR at 60% to 70%, depending on income level and the type of loan.

Here is a simple example. If your monthly net income is RM4,500 and you currently pay RM1,200 in existing loan instalments (car loan + personal loan), your current DSR is already 26.7%. If the new home loan adds another RM1,500 per month, your total DSR becomes 60% — right at the upper limit for many banks.

To lower your DSR before applying: pay off smaller loans ahead of schedule, reduce your credit card outstanding balance, and avoid taking on new credit commitments in the months before your application. Use our DSR calculator to check your exact ratio.

3 Pay Down Credit Card Balances

Many applicants overlook credit cards when calculating their DSR, but banks do not. In Malaysia, banks typically include 5% of your total credit card limit — or the actual minimum payment, whichever is higher — as a monthly commitment when calculating your DSR.

This means even if you rarely use a credit card, simply having a high limit can reduce your borrowing capacity. If you have two credit cards each with a RM20,000 limit, that could count as RM2,000 per month in notional commitments in some banks' calculations.

Consider requesting a limit reduction on cards you rarely use, or closing inactive accounts before applying for a major loan.

4 Check Your CTOS Score

While CCRIS shows your loan repayment history, CTOS gathers wider financial data including public records, legal judgments, and trade references. A good CTOS score (above 697 on their scale) signals to banks that you are a lower-risk borrower.

You can request a free MyCTOS Basic report or pay for a more detailed report at CTOS's official website. Review it for any legal judgments, outstanding PTPTN records, or trade references that could be affecting your score. Our guide on CTOS score and loan approval covers what banks look for in detail.

5 Stabilise Your Employment and Income

Banks prefer applicants who show consistent, documented income. For salaried employees, most banks require at least three to six months of continuous employment with the current employer. If you recently changed jobs — even to a higher-paying role — some banks may ask for a longer employment period before considering your application.

For self-employed borrowers, banks typically require two years of income documentation: a combination of business registration documents, bank statements, EPF statements, and income tax returns. If your income fluctuates significantly from month to month, banks will usually use a conservative average.

6 Avoid Multiple Applications at Once

Each time a bank or lender checks your CCRIS report, it leaves an "inquiry record." Too many inquiries in a short period — say, applying at five different banks within one month — can signal financial distress to lenders, even if you are simply shopping for the best rate.

A smarter approach is to use a mortgage broker or comparison tool to shortlist one or two banks first, then apply. If you need to compare offers, space out your applications where possible.

Realistic DSR Example: Before and After

Let us say Farah earns RM5,500 per month (net). She wants to apply for a home loan with a RM1,800 monthly instalment.

Before preparation:
Car loan: RM700 | Personal loan: RM400 | Credit card notional: RM500
Existing commitments: RM1,600 | New home loan: RM1,800
Total DSR: (RM1,600 + RM1,800) / RM5,500 = 61.8% — likely rejected or reduced

After preparation (settling personal loan):
Car loan: RM700 | Credit card notional: RM500
Existing commitments: RM1,200 | New home loan: RM1,800
Total DSR: (RM1,200 + RM1,800) / RM5,500 = 54.5% — within acceptable range

When AKPK Can Help

If your debt burden is already very high and you are struggling with repayments, applying for more loans is not the answer. AKPK (Agensi Kaunseling dan Pengurusan Kredit) offers free financial counselling and can help restructure existing debts through a Debt Management Programme. This might be a better first step before seeking new financing. Learn more in our AKPK guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the ideal DSR for loan approval in Malaysia?

Most Malaysian banks prefer a DSR of 60% or below, calculated on your net monthly income. Some banks allow up to 70% for higher-income earners (above RM10,000 per month), but a lower DSR gives you a stronger application and more negotiating room on the interest rate.

Does closing old credit cards improve loan approval chances?

It can help if those cards have high limits, because some banks count a percentage of your total credit limit as a monthly commitment when calculating DSR. However, closing accounts also reduces your total available credit, so it depends on your specific situation. Closing unused, high-limit cards is generally a smart move before a major loan application.

How long does a bad CCRIS record stay on file?

CCRIS typically retains repayment data for up to 12 months. So a late payment from more than a year ago will no longer appear on the active report, though it may take a few weeks after your account improves before the updated record is reflected across banking systems.

Can I get a loan approved if I have a CTOS record?

Having a CTOS record does not automatically disqualify you. It depends on the nature of the record. A judgment that has been settled and a supporting letter from the creditor can still lead to approval, particularly if your income and CCRIS history are strong. Banks look at the full picture, not just one data point.

Does applying for a loan affect my CCRIS?

Yes. Each bank inquiry on your CCRIS leaves a record. While one or two inquiries are normal, multiple inquiries in a short period can signal that you are urgently seeking credit, which may make banks more cautious. Try to limit how many banks you formally apply to within a 90-day window.

Is it better to settle all existing loans before applying for a home loan?

Not necessarily all of them — but settling high-commitment loans like personal loans or hire purchase agreements that are nearly finished can significantly improve your DSR. Focus on reducing total monthly commitments, not on clearing every single facility. Run the numbers using our DSR calculator first.
