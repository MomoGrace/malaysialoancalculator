Car Loan Settlement Malaysia: How Early Settlement Works

Last updated: May 2026  |  Reading time: ~7 min

Quick Answer Early settlement of a car loan in Malaysia allows you to pay off the remaining balance before the end of the agreed tenure. You will receive a settlement rebate on the unearned interest, calculated using the Rule of 78 method (for flat-rate hire purchase loans). To settle early, request a settlement letter from your bank or finance company, which will state the exact amount you need to pay. Settling early can reduce your total interest cost and improve your DSR for future loan applications.
This guide is for general information only. It is not financial advice and is not affiliated with any bank or government agency. Settlement amounts and procedures vary by lender. Always contact your bank directly for your exact settlement figure.

What Is Early Settlement for a Car Loan?

In Malaysia, car loans — formally called hire purchase agreements — are typically structured over three to nine years. When you take out the loan, the total interest for the full tenure is calculated upfront (because most car loans use a flat interest rate) and included in your total repayment amount.

Early settlement means you choose to pay off the remaining outstanding amount before the agreed end date. Because you are not using the full loan period, you are entitled to a rebate on the interest charges that have not yet been "earned" by the lender.

This rebate is not the same as getting all your future interest back — it is calculated using a specific formula called the Rule of 78.

Understanding the Rule of 78

The Rule of 78 (also called the Sum of Digits method) is the standard method used in Malaysia for calculating early settlement rebates on hire purchase loans, as mandated under the Hire Purchase Act 1967.

The name "Rule of 78" comes from the fact that the digits 1 to 12 add up to 78 (for a 12-month loan). The idea is that more interest is front-loaded into the early months of a loan — so when you settle early, the rebate you receive is smaller in the early years and larger closer to the end of the tenure.

How the Rebate Is Calculated (Simplified)

Here is a simplified illustration. Suppose you have a 5-year (60-month) car loan, and you want to settle after 30 months (halfway through):

  • Total interest on the loan: RM8,000
  • Months remaining at settlement: 30
  • The rebate fraction is calculated as: sum of remaining months ÷ sum of all months (1 to 60)
  • Sum of digits 1 to 60 = 1,830
  • Sum of digits 1 to 30 (remaining) ≈ 465
  • Rebate = RM8,000 × (465 ÷ 1,830) ≈ RM2,033

So your settlement amount would be the outstanding principal plus remaining interest, minus the RM2,033 rebate. This is a simplification — the actual bank calculation will be more precise. Always use the official settlement letter as your final figure.

Important: The Rule of 78 means settling in the first half of your loan tenure returns a smaller rebate than settling in the second half. The closer you are to the end of the loan, the better the rebate proportion.

Step-by-Step: How to Settle Your Car Loan Early in Malaysia

  1. Contact your bank or finance company. Call the customer service line or visit a branch. Request an early settlement quote or redemption statement for your car loan account.
  2. Request a settlement letter. The bank will issue an official settlement letter stating the total settlement amount, the rebate amount, and the validity period (usually 14 to 30 days).
  3. Verify the settlement amount. Cross-check the figure against your loan agreement and your own calculation. If anything looks unclear, ask the bank to explain the breakdown.
  4. Make payment before the expiry date. Pay the settlement amount by the date stated on the letter. Late payment beyond the validity period may require a new settlement letter.
  5. Obtain a release letter and road tax endorsement. After full settlement, the bank will provide a letter of release (also called a discharge of charge). You must bring this to JPJ (Jabatan Pengangkutan Jalan) to update the ownership records and remove the bank's caveat on your vehicle.
  6. Check your CCRIS. Within one to two months, your CCRIS record should reflect the closed account. Verify this by checking your CCRIS report.

Does Settling a Car Loan Early Actually Save Money?

The honest answer depends on how far you are into the loan tenure and what you plan to do with the money instead.

Example: Car Loan Settlement Calculation

Haziq purchased a car with a RM60,000 hire purchase loan at 3.5% flat rate over 7 years (84 months).

  • Total interest: RM60,000 × 3.5% × 7 = RM14,700
  • Total repayment: RM74,700
  • Monthly instalment: RM74,700 ÷ 84 = RM889

After 3 years (36 months paid), Haziq wants to settle. He has paid RM889 × 36 = RM32,004. He requests a settlement letter and the bank calculates a rebate of approximately RM4,800 using the Rule of 78. His settlement amount is roughly RM74,700 − RM32,004 − RM4,800 = RM37,896.

Without the rebate, he would have paid RM74,700 − RM32,004 = RM42,696 remaining. The rebate saves him nearly RM4,800.

Effect on Your Credit Profile

Once a car loan is fully settled and the account is closed, it will be reflected in your CCRIS report. A settled account with a clean payment history is generally positive — it shows you are capable of managing credit responsibly and completing a commitment.

More importantly for many borrowers, removing the car loan instalment from your monthly obligations reduces your DSR, which can directly improve your eligibility for a home loan or other credit. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get a settlement letter for my car loan in Malaysia?

Contact your bank or finance company — either by phone, online, or at a branch — and request an early settlement letter or redemption statement. Most banks can issue this within one to three working days. The letter will state the total settlement amount, the rebate given, and the validity date.

Will I save money by settling my car loan early?

You will receive a rebate on unearned interest using the Rule of 78 method. The further along you are in the loan tenure, the larger the rebate as a proportion of remaining interest. Settling in the final two years of a seven-year loan typically saves a meaningful amount. Settling in the first year saves less in absolute terms, but may still benefit your DSR.

What is the Rule of 78 in Malaysian car loans?

The Rule of 78 (or Sum of Digits) is a method for calculating how much unearned interest is rebated when a hire purchase loan is settled early. More interest is loaded into the early months of the loan, meaning the rebate is proportionally smaller when you settle early and larger when you settle later. It is the standard method under Malaysia's Hire Purchase Act 1967.

What documents do I need after settling my car loan?

After settling the loan, you need the bank's official release letter (discharge of charge). Bring this to JPJ to remove the bank's caveat on the vehicle ownership records. You should also keep a copy of your settlement receipt and the updated CCRIS report confirming the account is closed.

Does settling a car loan improve my CCRIS record?

A settled account with a clean payment history is viewed positively. The account will be marked as "settled" and the monthly commitment will no longer appear as an active obligation. This can improve your DSR calculation for future loan applications. Expect the CCRIS record to update within one to two months after settlement.

Can I settle my car loan if I have missed some payments?

Yes, but any outstanding arrears (missed payments plus late charges) will typically be added to your settlement amount. You cannot settle the loan while leaving outstanding arrears unpaid. Contact your bank to get the exact settlement figure, which will include all outstanding amounts.
