Public Bank and Home Loans in Malaysia

Public Bank Berhad is known among Malaysian borrowers for its competitive home loan rates and efficient processing. It is one of the more popular choices for home financing, particularly among salaried employees and long-term customers of the bank.

Like all Malaysian banks, Public Bank assesses each home loan application based on income, existing debt, credit history, and property details. Using a loan calculator before you apply gives you a clearer picture of what to expect.

Key Checks Before You Apply

1. Check Your DSR First

Public Bank uses your Debt Service Ratio to determine whether you can comfortably service the new loan alongside existing commitments. Add up all your monthly loan obligations (car loan, personal loan, credit card minimum payments) and divide by your gross income. If the result exceeds 60–70%, your application may be declined or offered at a lower loan amount.

Use the DSR calculator to check yours before applying.

2. Review Your CCRIS Report

CCRIS shows banks 12 months of your repayment behaviour across all credit facilities. Even one or two months of missed payments can affect your application. If you have any issues, address them before applying. Read the full CCRIS guide to understand what banks see.

3. Confirm Your Down Payment Readiness

A 10% down payment is typically required for a first or second residential property. But you should also budget for legal fees (approximately 1–2% of property price), stamp duty, valuation fees, and mortgage insurance. Together, these can add up to 3–5% of the property price on top of the down payment.

4. Verify the Property Type

Public Bank may have different terms for landed property versus strata-title units, and for subsale versus new launches. Confirm with the bank what margin of finance applies to your specific property. See our margin of finance guide.

Using the Loan Calculator Effectively

When using our home loan repayment calculator, enter:

  • The loan amount (property price minus your down payment)
  • An interest rate of 4.0%–4.5% as a planning range
  • Your desired tenure (25 or 30 years is common)

Try different scenarios. See how a 25-year versus 30-year tenure affects your monthly payment. A shorter tenure means higher monthly payments but less total interest paid over the loan's lifetime.

What Affects the Rate Public Bank Offers You

Public Bank's variable rate home loans are typically pegged to the base rate set by Bank Negara Malaysia's Overnight Policy Rate (OPR). Your final rate will be base rate plus a spread, which varies based on your risk profile, income, and the loan amount. Higher-value loans with strong credit profiles may attract more competitive pricing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Public Bank's home loan interest rate?

Public Bank's home loan rates are tied to the OPR and vary by borrower profile. As of mid-2025, indicative rates are typically in the 4.0%–4.5% p.a. range. Confirm the current rate directly with Public Bank.

Can I estimate my monthly repayment before applying?

Yes. Use a home loan calculator with your expected loan amount, an estimated interest rate, and your preferred tenure. This gives a working estimate to help you plan your budget.

Does Public Bank offer Islamic home financing?

Yes. Public Bank offers Islamic banking products under its Public Islamic Bank subsidiary. These are Shariah-compliant financing products. Speak with the bank for current product offerings.

What is the minimum salary to qualify for a Public Bank home loan?

There is no single minimum salary. Qualification depends on your DSR — the new loan's monthly repayment should fit within an acceptable DSR range after accounting for existing debts. Use the DSR calculator to check your position.

What happens if my property valuation is lower than the purchase price?

The bank bases the loan amount on the lower of the purchase price or the market valuation. If valuation is lower, your loan will be smaller and you will need to cover the shortfall from your own funds, on top of the down payment.
