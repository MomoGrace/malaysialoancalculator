CCRIS Report Malaysia: What Banks See Before Approving Your Loan

Last updated: May 2026  |  Reading time: ~7 min

Quick Answer CCRIS (Central Credit Reference Information System) is a database maintained by Bank Negara Malaysia that records your repayment history across all credit facilities at licensed financial institutions. When you apply for a loan, the bank checks your CCRIS to see whether you have been repaying existing loans on time. A clean CCRIS record improves your chances of approval; missed payments or outstanding defaults can lead to rejection.
This guide is for general information only. It is not financial advice and is not affiliated with Bank Negara Malaysia, CTOS, or any bank or government agency.

What Is CCRIS?

CCRIS — Central Credit Reference Information System — is operated by Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM), the country's central bank. It collects credit repayment data from all BNM-licensed financial institutions in Malaysia and consolidates this into a single report for each borrower.

Unlike a credit score (which is a single number), a CCRIS report presents raw repayment data across each credit facility you hold. Banks use this data to assess whether you are a responsible borrower.

What Does a CCRIS Report Contain?

A CCRIS report shows:

  • Outstanding credit facilities — all current loans and credit facilities, including home loans, car loans, personal loans, and credit cards
  • Monthly repayment status — whether each payment was made on time for the past 12 months
  • Special attention accounts — accounts flagged for arrears, restructuring, or rescheduling
  • Credit applications — recent loan application enquiries made by lenders in the last 12 months

How Repayment Status Is Recorded

CodeMeaning
0No arrears (paid on time)
11 month overdue
22 months overdue
33 months overdue
XNo data / account opened within the month

Banks typically flag applicants who have a status of "1" or higher for any credit facility in the past 6–12 months. Three or more consecutive months of arrears on any account is a serious red flag.

Why Banks Check CCRIS

Before a Malaysian bank approves any loan — home loan, car loan, or personal loan — it is required to check the borrower's CCRIS record as part of responsible lending practice under BNM guidelines. The bank wants to know:

  • Are you currently servicing all your existing debts on time?
  • What is your total outstanding debt across all institutions?
  • Have you recently applied for multiple loans simultaneously?

This information directly affects whether your loan is approved, and sometimes influences the interest rate offered.

How to Get Your Own CCRIS Report

You can request your own CCRIS report for free. Here's how:

Option 1: BNM's eCCRIS Portal
Visit the official eCCRIS portal at bnmlink.bnm.gov.my and log in with your MyKad details. Your report is generated immediately.
Option 2: BNM Kiosks
Self-service eCCRIS kiosks are available at Bank Negara Malaysia branches in Kuala Lumpur and other major cities.
Option 3: Via Your Bank
Some banks can pull a CCRIS enquiry with your consent as part of a loan pre-assessment.

It is advisable to check your CCRIS at least 3 months before applying for a major loan. This gives you time to identify and resolve issues such as late payments that have been incorrectly recorded, or to settle overdue accounts.

Common CCRIS Issues That Cause Loan Rejection

Late Payments

Even a single month of late payment on a credit card or personal loan shows up in your CCRIS and may concern a bank. Three or more consecutive late payments is a significant red flag. The data remains visible for 12 months from the last update.

High Outstanding Balances

Banks look at your total outstanding credit commitments relative to your income. High balances across multiple facilities push up your DSR, potentially causing a new loan to be declined. Check your DSR to see where you stand.

Restructured or Rescheduled Accounts

Accounts that have been restructured or rescheduled due to financial difficulty are flagged in the "special attention" section of CCRIS. Banks treat these with caution, especially within 12 months of the restructuring.

Too Many Recent Enquiries

Multiple loan applications in a short period generate multiple CCRIS enquiries. This can suggest financial distress and may reduce your chances of approval at any one bank.

Improving Your CCRIS Standing

The most effective steps are also the simplest:

  1. Pay all instalments and minimum credit card payments before the due date.
  2. Set up auto-debit to avoid missing payments due to forgetfulness.
  3. Settle any overdue accounts as soon as possible.
  4. Reduce credit card balances to below 50% of your credit limit where possible.
  5. Avoid applying for multiple loans at once — space out applications.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CCRIS the same as a credit score?

No. CCRIS is a raw repayment data report, not a scored system. It shows monthly payment status for each credit facility. Your CTOS score is a credit score derived from CCRIS data plus other sources. See our CTOS score guide for more.

How long does late payment data stay on CCRIS?

CCRIS shows the past 12 months of repayment data. Once 12 months have passed since a late payment, it rolls off the report. Data is updated monthly by the reporting financial institution.

Can I dispute errors in my CCRIS report?

Yes. If you find inaccurate information — for example, a payment recorded as overdue when you have proof of payment — you can raise a dispute with the relevant financial institution that reported the data. They are required to update CCRIS with corrected information.

Does checking my own CCRIS affect my loan eligibility?

No. Checking your own CCRIS report does not generate a lender enquiry and does not affect your credit standing.

What is the difference between CCRIS and CTOS?

CCRIS is run by Bank Negara Malaysia and shows repayment history across licensed banks only. CTOS is a private credit agency that compiles a broader profile including CCRIS data, legal records, and public information. Both are used during loan assessments.
