Home Loan Margin of Finance Malaysia: What 90% Financing Means

Last updated: May 2026  |  Reading time: ~7 min

Quick Answer The margin of finance (MOF) is the percentage of a property's purchase price or market value that a bank will lend you. In Malaysia, first and second residential properties typically qualify for up to 90% financing, meaning you need at least a 10% down payment from your own funds. For a third property, Bank Negara Malaysia's ruling caps MOF at 70%, requiring a 30% down payment. The actual margin approved depends on your income, credit profile, and property valuation.

What Is Margin of Finance?

When you apply for a home loan in Malaysia, the bank does not typically lend you the full purchase price of the property. The amount the bank is willing to lend, expressed as a percentage of the property value or purchase price, is called the margin of finance (MOF).

A 90% MOF means the bank lends up to 90% of the property value. You are responsible for funding the remaining 10% as a down payment from your own savings. A 70% MOF means the bank lends only 70%, and you need to come up with 30% yourself.

Understanding your margin of finance is critical because it directly determines how much cash you need to have ready before you can complete a property purchase.

Bank Negara Malaysia's MOF Rules

Bank Negara Malaysia (BNM) introduced a property financing policy to reduce speculative buying and manage household debt levels. The key rules that apply to residential properties are:

Property CountMaximum MOFMinimum Down Payment
First residential propertyUp to 90%At least 10%
Second residential propertyUp to 90%At least 10%
Third residential property (and beyond)Up to 70%At least 30%

The count refers to the number of outstanding home loans you have at the time of application — not the total number of properties you have ever owned. If you have fully settled and closed a previous home loan, it may not count toward the limit depending on how the bank assesses your record.

Note: The 70% cap on the third property applies regardless of property type — landed or high-rise, completed or under construction. Some affordable housing schemes may have different government-backed financing arrangements.

Purchase Price vs Market Value: Which Does the Bank Use?

This is a common source of confusion for first-time buyers. The margin of finance is applied to whichever is lower between the purchase price and the bank's independent valuation of the property.

For example, if you are buying a property at RM550,000 but the bank's appointed valuer assesses the market value at RM520,000, the bank will calculate 90% of RM520,000 (RM468,000) — not 90% of the purchase price. This means you would need to fund the RM30,000 difference plus the 10% down payment from your own cash.

Example Calculation

Siti is buying a condominium in Selangor:

  • Purchase price: RM480,000
  • Bank valuation: RM460,000 (lower of the two)
  • Margin of finance: 90%
  • Maximum loan amount: RM460,000 × 90% = RM414,000
  • Amount Siti needs to fund: RM480,000 − RM414,000 = RM66,000 cash
  • Monthly repayment on RM414,000 at 4.25% over 30 years: ~RM2,037

Siti needs at least RM66,000 in cash upfront — which is significantly more than just the 10% down payment on the purchase price (RM48,000). This is why property buyers are often advised to budget for at least 15% of the purchase price in upfront cash to cover both the down payment shortfall and other purchase costs.

What Reduces the Approved Margin of Finance?

Even for a first or second property, the bank may offer a lower margin than the maximum 90%. Several factors can reduce your approved MOF:

1. High Debt Service Ratio (DSR)

If your existing monthly commitments already consume a large portion of your income, the bank may reduce the loan amount — and therefore the effective MOF — to bring the monthly instalment within an acceptable DSR range. Use our DSR calculator to check your ratio before applying.

2. Weak Credit History

Borrowers with CCRIS records showing late payments or defaults may receive a lower margin offer. Banks price in the higher perceived risk by lending less. Learn more in our CCRIS report guide.

3. Property Type and Age

Older properties, leasehold properties with short remaining tenure, or properties in lower-demand areas may receive a lower MOF from some banks due to higher collateral risk.

4. Income Documentation

Self-employed borrowers or those whose income cannot be fully documented may receive a lower margin of finance compared to salaried employees with payslips and EPF statements.

Tip for buyers: Always get a property valuation done through the bank's panel valuer before committing to a purchase. The valuation gap (between purchase price and valuation) is a cost many first-time buyers are not prepared for.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I get 100% home loan financing in Malaysia?

In general, no. Standard home loans in Malaysia offer up to 90% MOF for first and second properties. However, some government-backed schemes — such as PR1MA, MyHome, or specific bank programmes for first-time buyers — may offer higher financing ratios with subsidised structures. Eligibility criteria apply and these are not offered by all banks or for all property types.

What happens if the bank values my property lower than the purchase price?

The bank lends based on the lower of the purchase price or the bank's valuation. If there is a gap, you must fund that difference in cash on top of your down payment. This is called a "valuation shortfall" and is one of the most common surprises for first-time home buyers in Malaysia.

Does EPF withdrawal count toward the down payment?

Yes. EPF (Employees Provident Fund) Account 2 withdrawals can be used to fund the down payment and other property purchase costs, including stamp duty and legal fees. However, the availability of funds depends on your EPF balance and the type of property (first home, second home, etc.). Check current EPF guidelines for eligibility.

If I sell my first home, can I get 90% financing for a new purchase?

It depends on whether the first home loan is fully settled and closed at the time of application. If you have settled the previous home loan and have no outstanding home loan, most banks will treat your new purchase as effectively a first loan, making you eligible for up to 90% MOF — but always confirm with the bank's credit team.

Is the 70% cap on the third property strictly enforced?

Yes. Bank Negara Malaysia's Third Property Guidelines have been in place since 2010 and banks are required to follow them. The 70% cap applies to the third and any subsequent residential property purchase where you still have two active outstanding home loans. There are no known exceptions for general borrowers.

Can a joint loan application improve the approved margin of finance?

A joint application combines the income of both applicants, which can improve the overall DSR and potentially lead to a higher loan amount being approved. However, the margin of finance cap (90% or 70%) remains the same — the limit is based on the number of outstanding home loans, not the income level of the applicants.
