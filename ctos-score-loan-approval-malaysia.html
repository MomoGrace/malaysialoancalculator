How CTOS Score Can Affect Loan Approval in Malaysia

Last updated: May 2026  |  Reading time: ~7 min

Quick Answer Your CTOS score is a credit score derived from public records, CCRIS data, and other financial information. In Malaysia, banks and lenders may use this score as part of the loan approval process. A higher CTOS score (generally above 650) indicates lower credit risk and improves your chances of loan approval. A low score can result in rejection or higher interest rates offered.
This guide is for general information only. It is not financial advice and is not affiliated with CTOS Data Systems Sdn Bhd or any bank or government agency. CTOS score usage policies may vary by lender.

What Is a CTOS Score?

CTOS Data Systems is a private credit reporting agency licensed by the Ministry of Finance Malaysia. The company compiles credit information from multiple sources — including CCRIS data (bank repayment history), legal records (bankruptcies, judgments), trade references, and public records — into a single credit profile.

The CTOS score is a numerical representation of your creditworthiness. It gives lenders a quick indicator of how likely you are to repay a new loan based on your historical financial behaviour.

Most lenders prefer borrowers with a score of 650 or above. Scores below 550 may lead to automatic rejection at some institutions, while scores in the 550–649 range may result in conditional approval or a lower loan amount being offered.

What Affects Your CTOS Score?

Several factors influence your CTOS score. Understanding them helps you know what to improve:

1. Repayment History (CCRIS Data)

This is the biggest driver. Late or missed payments on any credit facility — car loan, credit card, personal loan, home loan — negatively affect your score. Banks report repayment data to CCRIS monthly, and CTOS pulls this data into your profile. Even one month of late payment can leave a mark for up to 12 months.

2. Legal Records and Judgments

If you have been named in a civil suit for debt recovery, or if a court judgment has been entered against you, this shows up in your CTOS report. Bankruptcy declarations are also recorded. These are among the most serious negative factors.

3. Outstanding Debts and Utilisation

High credit card utilisation (using close to your full credit limit) and multiple active loans signal financial strain. Keeping balances manageable relative to your credit limit can help protect your score.

4. CTOS Enquiries

Whenever a lender pulls your CTOS report for a loan application, it is recorded. Too many enquiries in a short period can suggest financial desperation, which may lower your score slightly.

5. Length of Credit History

A longer, clean credit history — for example, a car loan repaid without issues over five years — builds your credibility. Thin credit files (no credit history at all) can also result in lower scores because there is nothing to base an assessment on.

Can You Check Your Own CTOS Score?

Yes. You can request your own CTOS report and score through the CTOS website or mobile app. Checking your own report does not affect your score. MyCTOS Basic is a free basic report, while MyCTOS Score provides the full credit score and a more detailed report for a fee.

It is advisable to check your CTOS report at least 3–6 months before applying for a major loan. This gives you time to identify and resolve any issues — such as disputing inaccurate records — before lenders see your profile.

How to Improve Your CTOS Score

  1. Pay on time, every time. Set up auto-debit for all loan and credit card payments.
  2. Reduce outstanding balances. Pay down credit card debt, especially any accounts near the credit limit.
  3. Resolve any legal disputes or judgments. If there are court judgments or bankruptcy proceedings, address these with legal help before applying for new credit.
  4. Avoid applying for multiple loans simultaneously. Space out your credit applications to avoid too many CTOS enquiries.
  5. Dispute errors. If your CTOS report contains inaccurate information, raise a dispute with CTOS directly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good CTOS score for a home loan in Malaysia?

Generally, a score of 650 or above is considered good. Scores of 700 and above give you a stronger position when applying for home loans. However, each lender has its own threshold — confirm with your bank what score they require.

Does checking my own CTOS report affect my score?

No. Checking your own CTOS report (a "soft inquiry") does not affect your score. Only lender inquiries during a loan application (a "hard inquiry") may have a small impact.

How long does negative information stay on my CTOS report?

The retention period varies by record type. CCRIS data (bank loan payment history) is retained for 12 months from the last update. Legal records and judgments may stay longer. Check with CTOS for specific retention policies.

All Malaysian banks use CTOS?

Not all banks use CTOS in the same way. Some banks check CTOS as part of their standard process, others rely more heavily on CCRIS, and some use both. Either way, maintaining a clean credit profile helps across all checks.

What can I do if my CTOS report has errors?

You can raise a dispute directly with CTOS Data Systems. Provide supporting documents showing the information is incorrect. CTOS is required to investigate and correct inaccurate records within a reasonable period.
