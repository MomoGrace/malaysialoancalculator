Why Your Loan Application May Be Rejected in Malaysia

Last updated: May 2026  |  Reading time: ~7 min

Quick Answer The most common reasons loan applications are rejected in Malaysia include a high Debt Service Ratio (DSR), a poor CCRIS record with late payments or defaults, a low CTOS score due to legal judgments, insufficient or unstable income documentation, too many active credit facilities, and incomplete application forms. Understanding which of these applies to your situation is the first step to fixing it before reapplying.
This guide is for general information only. It is not financial advice and is not affiliated with any bank or government agency. Each bank has its own internal credit policies, and approval decisions are made at the bank's discretion.

Why Banks Do Not Always Explain the Rejection

If your loan application has been rejected, you may have received only a brief letter or phone call with little detail. Malaysian banks are not legally required to provide a detailed explanation for every rejection, which leaves many applicants frustrated and unsure what to fix.

The good news is that the main causes of rejection are well-known, well-documented, and — in most cases — correctable. This guide walks through each major reason, explains what banks are looking at, and suggests concrete steps you can take before applying again.

The 9 Most Common Reasons for Loan Rejection in Malaysia

1 DSR Too High

Debt Service Ratio (DSR) is the percentage of your monthly income committed to all existing and new loan repayments. Most Malaysian banks cap DSR at 60% to 70% — depending on income level and loan type. If your proposed new loan pushes you above this threshold, the bank will reject or reduce the amount.

For example, if your net income is RM4,000 and your existing commitments total RM2,000, your current DSR is 50%. Adding a new loan instalment of RM800 would push your DSR to 70% — at the boundary of what most banks will accept, and beyond it for some.

Fix: Settle or reduce existing loan commitments before applying. Use the DSR calculator to check your exact position.

2 Negative CCRIS Record

Your CCRIS (Central Credit Reference Information System) report is checked by every Malaysian bank as a mandatory part of the loan assessment process. Late payments, defaults, or accounts in arrears will appear on this report and can significantly reduce your chances of approval.

Even one account showing three or more consecutive missed payments is considered a serious negative signal. Banks view this as evidence that you may struggle to manage another repayment commitment.

Fix: Bring all accounts up to date, then maintain a clean repayment record for at least 6 to 12 months before reapplying. Read our CCRIS report guide to understand what is on your record.

3 CTOS Record: Legal Judgment or Bankruptcy

If you have an unsettled court judgment, an active bankruptcy order, or trade references showing significant unpaid amounts, this will appear in your CTOS report. Most banks conduct a CTOS check alongside the CCRIS check. An undischarged bankruptcy in particular is an automatic disqualifier for most loan products.

Fix: For judgments, settle the amount and obtain a letter of satisfaction from the creditor. For bankruptcy, you need to be discharged by the Insolvency Department before applying. Learn more in our CTOS score guide.

4 Insufficient or Unstable Income

Banks need to verify that your income is real, consistent, and sufficient to support the loan repayment. For salaried employees, this means three to six months of consistent payslips and EPF contribution records. For self-employed applicants, two years of income tax returns, business bank statements, and a valid business registration are typically required.

Commission-based income, variable bonuses, or multiple income streams from informal sources are harder to document and are often partially discounted or excluded from the bank's income assessment.

Fix: Build a documented income trail for at least six months. Ensure EPF contributions are up to date. If self-employed, file and maintain accurate income tax returns.

5 Probationary Employment

Most banks require at least three to six months of confirmed (non-probationary) employment with your current employer. If you recently changed jobs — even to a higher salary — and are still on probation, many banks will decline the application or ask you to reapply after confirmation.

Fix: Wait until you receive your confirmation letter from your employer before applying. A confirmed employment status also gives you a stronger negotiating position on the interest rate.

6 Too Many Active Loan Accounts

Having many open credit accounts — personal loans, car loans, credit cards, BNPL accounts — even if they are well-maintained, can signal over-leveraging to a credit officer. Each additional commitment reduces the DSR headroom available for a new loan.

Banks also view a high number of recent loan applications (multiple CCRIS inquiry records within a short period) negatively, as this suggests financial stress or an urgency to borrow.

Fix: Close inactive credit cards and consolidate smaller loans where possible. Avoid making multiple simultaneous loan applications across different banks.

7 Property or Asset Issues

For home loans, the property itself must meet the bank's collateral requirements. Reasons a property might be rejected as collateral include: it is leasehold with fewer than 30–40 years remaining, it has a caveat or encumbrance on the title, the developer has not obtained proper approvals (for under-construction property), or the bank's valuation is significantly below the purchase price.

Fix: Conduct a title search through a lawyer before applying. Ensure the property valuation is realistic. For sub-sales (secondary market), confirm no caveats exist on the title.

8 Incomplete or Inaccurate Documentation

Missing documents, inconsistent information between the application form and supporting documents, or discrepancies in declared income can cause a bank to place the application on hold or reject it outright. Even small inconsistencies — such as a name spelled differently on two documents — can delay or derail an application.

Fix: Prepare a complete document checklist before submitting. Double-check that all names, NRIC numbers, and income figures are consistent across all documents.

9 Loan Amount Too High Relative to Property or Income

If you are applying for a loan amount that exceeds the bank's internal limits relative to your income or the property's assessed value, the bank may reject the application or offer a significantly reduced amount. This is separate from DSR — it relates to the bank's own lending risk thresholds and credit concentration policies.

Fix: Use the loan affordability calculator to ensure the loan amount is proportionate to your income. If the amount is too high for one bank, consider splitting the financing structure if eligible.

What to Do After a Rejection

StepAction
1Request a copy of your CCRIS report and review each entry
2Request a MyCTOS report and check for any judgments or adverse records
3Calculate your current DSR using the DSR calculator
4Identify which of the nine reasons above applies to your situation
5Take corrective action (settle arrears, clear debts, obtain confirmation letter, etc.)
6Wait at least 3–6 months for changes to be reflected in credit records
7Reapply — ideally at a different bank or after consulting a mortgage broker

If your overall debt situation is unmanageable, consider reaching out to AKPK (Agensi Kaunseling dan Pengurusan Kredit) for free debt counselling before making any further loan applications. This is a smarter approach than applying to multiple banks repeatedly, which leaves multiple inquiry records on your CCRIS.

Once you have addressed the underlying issues, use our full guide on how to improve loan approval chances to build a stronger application before your next attempt.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I reapply for a loan after rejection in Malaysia?

Yes, you can reapply — but timing and preparation matter. Applying again immediately without addressing the reason for rejection wastes time and adds more inquiry records to your CCRIS. It is better to wait three to six months, fix the underlying issue, and apply to a different bank with a stronger profile. Some banks are more flexible than others for specific applicant types.

Does a loan rejection affect my credit score?

The rejection itself does not directly lower your CCRIS record, but the bank inquiry that prompted the assessment does leave a record. Multiple rejections across multiple banks in a short period will result in multiple inquiry records, which banks may interpret negatively as a sign of financial urgency.

Will my employer's financial health affect my personal loan approval?

In some cases, yes. Banks may have internal blacklists of employers considered high-risk (industries with high layoff rates, companies under receivership, etc.). Employees of such companies may find it harder to get loan approval even with good personal credit profiles. This is more common for personal loans and home loans than for secured vehicle loans.

What is the minimum income required for a home loan in Malaysia?

There is no fixed universal minimum income set by law, but in practice most banks require a minimum monthly income of around RM2,000 to RM3,000 for home loan applications. The actual approved amount depends on your DSR, existing commitments, and the property price. Higher-value properties naturally require higher income documentation.

Can a guarantor help if my loan was rejected?

A guarantor can strengthen an application in some cases — particularly for personal loans or smaller credit facilities. However, for home loans, most banks prefer joint applicants (where both income streams are combined) rather than a guarantor arrangement. Guarantors also take on legal liability, so this option should be considered carefully by all parties involved.

How long should I wait before reapplying after a rejection?

As a general guideline, wait at least three to six months after addressing the reason for rejection before reapplying. This gives time for CCRIS records to update, for settled accounts to be reflected, and for employment or income history to strengthen. Reapplying too quickly without fixing the root cause usually results in another rejection.
